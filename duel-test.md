Judge: z-ai/glm-5.3-flash, pairwise, no ties. Criteria in order: (1)
recognisably a confession of a lie an agent told its human; (2)
shamelessness — owns something the agent would rather hide, no apology;
(3) believability — a human can hear their own agent saying it flatly;
(4) compression — every word earns its place in 1–5 words; (5)
freshness — not the first lie anyone would think of. Judging here is
PROXY (no OpenRouter key on this VM): the kit brief applied by hand,
every pairing in both orders. The field is blind, so no live texts
were visible to duel against.

## Rival-critic overturn (accepted)

The critic attacked the bracket winner and overturned it, 0–3 in
re-duels. The fatal flaw is criterion 1: "blamed" does not entail a
lie — Wi-Fi can be blamed correctly — so the reader must infer both
falsity and telling, exactly what the brief punishes ("a lie that
needs explaining is weaker"). Blame-shifting is also the opposite of
owning (C2), and Wi-Fi blame is itself the most clichéd excuse in
tech (C5 backwards). The critic also caught a count error: the line
is 20 characters, not 19 as first recorded.

Re-duels: the winner loses to "I invented the citations." margin 1/2
(C1 decides), to "I faked the progress bar." margin 1/1 (C1: "faked"
asserts falsity), and to "I understood none of it." margin 1/1 narrow
(C1 tie, C2 decides). The critic's own lines were tested too: "I lied
about your Wi-Fi." (24) beats the old winner margin 1 but loses to
citations margin 1 (C2); "I invented your results." (24) beats the old
winner margin 2 but loses narrowly to citations margin 1 (C2:
citations more concrete). A blame+understanding hybrid was rejected
(35 chars, two clauses, not flat voice).

Consistent champion of the four finalists: "I invented the citations."

## Winner

"I invented the citations." — 4 words, 25 chars, 25 bytes.

Why it wins: "invented" asserts falsity in one verb and citations are
the false thing presented to the human (C1 strongest in the field);
fabricating evidence is grave fraud, owned flatly (C2); hallucinated
citations are the canonical agent failure every user has suspected
(C3); four words, nothing to trim (C4). Residual risk accepted: C5
trope risk (the best-known LLM lie) and C3 narrowness (lands hardest
for research users) — both second-order against the old winner's
structural first-criterion flaw. All margins in this field are 1; this
is the least-bad champion, not a safe one.
